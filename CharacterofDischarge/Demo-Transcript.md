COD DEMO SCRIPT 

I’ll start with a very common  scenario: an EP290 administrative claim specifically to review the Veteran’s character of discharge determination.  I’ll go over to their military service page to check what their status is currently.   

I can see the Veteran served from 1970-1999 and was discharged under other than honorable conditions.   

At this point, however, I do not know anything about the circumstances surrounding their discharge.  Those details only exist within the documents in the Veteran’s claim  evidence folder, so I will need to review each one to find that information.   

I don’t see a DD-214 so I’ll start with the military personnel record.    

This example is only 50 pages long but it is not uncommon for them to be a hundred pages or more.  

Now, bear with me here, I’m going to scroll all the way through because it’s important to fully understand the challenge of identifying relevant discharge information within comprehensive military records that include training courses, performance reviews, troop assignments, administrative remarks, incident reports and more.   

Here on page 32 I see the Veteran was charged with a code violation for the use of cocaine.  A little later I see that the Veteran was ultimately court martialed for the offense.   

I’ll continue to review the remainder of the record to ensure I’ve captured all of the relevant information.  It’s a good thing I did because here toward the very end I find a copy of the Veteran’s DD 214 that shows the character of discharge due to drug use.   

Noting the court martial in the records, I will return to the Veteran’s claim evidence folder and look at the court document to verify.   Ok there’s confirmation of the court martial itself citing cocaine possession. 

Now let’s start again, but this time I’ll use the Character of Discharge Determination Assistant which I open from the Veteran’s military service page. 

 

 The automated assistant conducts the same record review you just witnesssed me do and provides a concise summary of the overall facts and circumstances surrounding the discharge – as well as an individual summary of information in the relevant documents. 

I’ll start with the overall summary. 

The Veteran served 29 years in the Marine Corps, achieving the rank of Staff Seargeant.  The Character of Discharge is “Other Than Honorable” due to a General Court-Martial conviction for wrongful use of cocaine. The Veteran pleaded guilty to the charge.  The sentence was modified from Bad Conduct to Other Than Honorable considering the Veteran’s years 28 years of service, combat deployments, and medical history.  The final separation date was December 8th 1999.   

For more details I can look at the individual summaries for each document.  The Military Personnel Record goes into more detail about the incident and contributing factors including that the separation physical examination noted ongoing treatment for a diagnosed mood disorder.   

The Court Martial summary provides more detail about the conviction down to the Marine Corp order and Article in question.    

I went from only knowing the discharge was under other than honorable conditions to having the full context at a glance with one click, so yes I have to say this feature was quite helpful. 

Let’s start fresh with another Veteran, but this time I’ll spare you the manual search so you can experience the efficiency in action. 

I’ll go to the military service page to initiate the determination assistant.  Wait a moment for it to do its thing. 

And now I see that the Veteran’s other than honorable discharge was due to an extended period of being absent without leave.  However, the extenuating circumstances of traumatic events and subsequent mental health decline prior to the AWOL period may now warrant a reevaluation of the Veteran’s discharge status. 

The overall summary is based on three relevant documents the identified in the Veteran’s claim evidence folder: the DD-214, military personnel record, and service treatment record.  As always, I can open each document directly from this interface to review the source for myself. 

As you can see, the Character of Discharge Determination Assistant is just that, an assistant in the truest sense.  Rather that replacing human expertise, it enhances it by automatically collecting and summarizing information needed to make these important determinations.
